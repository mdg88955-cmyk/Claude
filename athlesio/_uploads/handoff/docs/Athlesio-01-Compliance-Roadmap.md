**Recommendation: launch UK first, US second, Canada last or never.** Canada is where dropshipped supplements go to die — the licensing sits with the product and the importer, neither of which you control in a Zendrop model.

## 1. The product risk you should look at before the regulatory one

Creatine gummies as a category failed independent testing badly in 2025.

- NOW Foods commissioned testing of 12 popular creatine gummy products — **46% failure rate** against label claim.
- One top-selling brand claiming 5 g creatine per serving contained **0.005 g** — essentially none.
- SuppCo's ISO 17025-accredited lab tested six gummies; **four contained virtually no creatine**.
- A UK-led test of nine brands found **five had under 0.1 g per serving**.

The chemistry explains it: creatine is stable as a dry powder, but gummies are made with water, and creatine in water converts to creatinine. Even a correctly dosed batch degrades on the shelf.

Your product claims 5,000 mg creatine in a 3-gummy serving — about 1.67 g per gummy. A typical gummy weighs 2–4 g total, so creatine would be roughly half the gummy's mass. That is not impossible, but it is at the edge of what is physically plausible, and it is exactly the claim profile that failed testing.

**Before you spend a euro on compliance:** get a **Certificate of Analysis from an ISO 17025 lab, on a recent batch, testing creatine content specifically.** Not a supplier-issued CoA — an independent one. If Zendrop can't produce it, that answers the question for you. FTC and ASA both treat a label overstatement as a false claim regardless of whether you knew.

Cost: roughly €200–500 for a single-analyte test. It is the cheapest decision-quality information available to you.

## 2. 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

### What's required

**Responsible Food Business Operator on the label.** The name and business address of the FBO must appear on the pack. If your business is established in Austria and you sell into GB, you need a **UK address** on the label. A UK-based fulfilment or compliance agent can serve this role.

**Labelling (retained EU FIC rules):** legal product name, ingredient list in descending weight order, allergens emphasised, net quantity, best-before, storage, batch code, "food supplement" designation, recommended daily portion, a "do not exceed the stated dose" statement, a "keep out of reach of young children" statement, and a statement that supplements are not a substitute for a varied diet.

**Nutrition and health claims — the important part.** Only claims on the **Great Britain Nutrition and Health Claims (NHC) register** may be used. On **27 February 2025** the register was updated to add two *non-authorised* claims, one of which (004UKNHCC) is for **creatine**.

**Practical effect: you may not make creatine performance or muscle claims in GB commercial communications.** Not on the product page, not in ads, not in influencer briefs. The wording "increases physical performance" that you may have seen on EU packs does not transfer to GB.

You *can* still make authorised claims for the other actives — notably **vitamin B6 and B12** ("contributes to normal energy-yielding metabolism", "contributes to the reduction of tiredness and fatigue"), provided the product meets the significant-amount threshold. Your B6 (5 mg) and B12 (50 µg) both clear it comfortably. **This is the compliant angle for your UK copy.**

**Advertising:** the ASA/CAP Code applies to your website and paid ads. Claims must not be false, ambiguous, or misleading, must not encourage excess consumption, and must not imply a balanced diet is inadequate.

### Action list

- [ ] Appoint a UK Responsible Person / get a UK address on the label
- [ ] Rewrite label and all copy to remove creatine health claims
- [ ] Build claims around authorised B6/B12 claims instead
- [ ] Confirm allergen and ingredient declarations match the actual formulation
- [ ] Register with the local authority where the UK FBO address sits, if you hold stock

## 3. 🇺🇸 United States

### What's required

**FDA facility registration.** Every domestic and foreign facility that manufactures, processes, packs, or holds dietary supplements for US import must register with FDA. This is the **manufacturer's** obligation, not yours — but if the facility isn't registered, your shipments are detained.

**US Agent.** A foreign facility must designate a US Agent who physically resides in the US and is reachable 24 hours a day for FDA communications and inspection scheduling. If FDA can't reach the agent, registration is suspended and **all your products go on automatic import hold**.

**Registration renewal** runs every two years, 1 October–31 December of even-numbered years. There is no grace period — a lapsed registration renders products adulterated.

**Supplement Facts panel** meeting 21 CFR 101.36, with a US-format ingredient list and the standard DSHEA disclaimer for any structure/function claim:
> *"These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."*

**FTC substantiation.** Any performance claim needs competent and reliable scientific evidence *for your product as formulated*. Creatine monohydrate has strong evidence as a powder at ~3–5 g/day; that evidence does not automatically transfer to a gummy that may not deliver the dose. See section 1.

**California Prop 65** warning obligations if you ship to CA.

**Coming:** the Dietary Supplement Listing Act of 2026 (H.R. 8370) was introduced 20 April 2026 and would create the first mandatory FDA product-listing regime for supplements. Watch it; it is not yet law.

### Action list

- [ ] Get the manufacturer's FDA facility registration number in writing
- [ ] Confirm a US Agent is appointed and current
- [ ] Obtain a compliant Supplement Facts panel image
- [ ] Add the DSHEA disclaimer to every page carrying a structure/function claim
- [ ] Add Prop 65 handling for CA orders
- [ ] Hold an independent CoA on file before making any dose claim

## 4. 🇨🇦 Canada — why I'd skip it

Creatine supplements are **Natural Health Products**. Unlike the US, Canada requires **pre-market approval**:

1. The product needs a **Natural Product Number (NPN)** issued by Health Canada after review for safety, efficacy and quality. The NPN must appear on the label. No NPN, no legal sale.
2. Separately, the product must be **imported by a site-licensed importer** responsible for lot release, recordkeeping and GMP compliance before sale.

Both attach to the product and the supply chain — neither is something you can add on top of a Zendrop dropship. Health Canada actively enforces: there are public recalls and licence suspensions for creatine products specifically.

**Realistic paths to Canada:**
- Find a Canadian distributor who already holds an NPN for an equivalent product and sell theirs
- Move to a private-label manufacturer who will run the NPN submission (months, and thousands of euros)
- Don't sell to Canada

**In Shopify, this means: do not enable Canada as a market.** I've left it out of the recommended setup for that reason.

## 5. Practical Shopify consequences

- **Shopify Payments and supplements.** Supplement merchants are elevated-risk for processors. Have a backup gateway identified before launch, and keep your claims conservative — aggressive claims are a common trigger for account review.
- **Markets setup** (your Basic plan supports this): keep Austria/EUR primary, add **United Kingdom / GBP** and **United States / USD** as additional markets with their own pricing and shipping zones. Leave Canada off.
- **Duties and import fees.** For US orders under the de minimis threshold and UK orders, check current thresholds before promising "no extra fees" — this is the single most common cause of refused deliveries and chargebacks for cross-border supplement sellers.
- **Age gating.** Not legally required for creatine in these markets, but sensible: add "not suitable for under 18s, pregnant or breastfeeding women" to the label and page.

---

## 6. What I'd do next, in order

1. **Get the independent CoA.** Everything else is wasted spend if the gummies don't contain what the label says.
2. **Launch UK only**, with claims built on B6/B12 and a UK Responsible Person.
3. **Add the US** once you have the manufacturer's FDA registration and US Agent confirmed in writing.
4. **Revisit Canada** only if you move off dropshipping to a private label with an NPN.
